The uploaded daemon removes the stellar model
It sets:

_stellar_model_jl = nothing

Consequently,

has_stars = stellar_model !== nothing && ML > 0.0

is always false. M/L cannot affect the gravitational potential. This could directly explain the completely flat M/L direction we saw. Karl halo parameters and explicit velocity edges are also discarded.

The orbit library is intentionally incomplete

fill_pct=0.80 stops construction after only 80% of the base-orbit slots succeed. Remaining columns stay zero. A timeout or lower fill can still reach the weight solver without being rejected.

The missing fraction is not neutral. Orbits are attempted in an ordered sequence.

The circular family cannot launch correctly

The declared angular-momentum grid includes:

Lfrac = (0.05, 0.2, 0.4, 0.7, 1.0)

For Lfrac=1.0, the code calculates circular-orbit energy at rapo. It then starts inside that radius with the same energy and angular momentum. In a spherical potential that location is forbidden, giving a negative radial-velocity argument. This likely explains why the code was allowed to stop at 80%: roughly one of the five families cannot launch.

That leaves a radially biased library.

Full-light constraints can exceed orbit coverage

The outermost orbit shell comes from:

shells = sort(copy(R_star_m[isfinite.(R_star_m)]))

The light target comes from independent light_edges. If the full-light profile extends farther than the stellar-radius sample, outer light rows exist without any orbit capable of filling them.

The repaired expanded solver should reject that impossible system. The old behavior could hide it.

Orbit evaluation is not deterministic

Random launch radii depend on thread assignment and evaluation order. Helper threads can also attempt the same orbit slot concurrently. The same physical parameter point can produce a different matrix when its batch position changes.

That noise can imitate small parameter sensitivity. In the current daemon, apparent differences between ml_up and ml_down could be entirely numerical because stellar gravity is absent.

Two force options are unsafe as implemented

The normal spherical nfw and spherical nonsingular_isothermal equations look physically and dimensionally consistent.

Two optional paths do not:

karl_halo density functions expect radii in parsecs. They receive meters.
For flattened halos, force comes from an axisymmetric table while potential remains spherical. Orbit launch energy and integrated force no longer describe the same gravitational field.

Those modes should be blocked until repaired.

The repair order should be:

Restore the stellar model, velocity edges, and Karl halo parameters at the daemon boundary.
Make orbit construction deterministic and require a genuinely complete library.
Repair the circular-orbit launch plus extend shell coverage through the full-light range.
Test force–potential agreement, orbit energy conservation, matrix column coverage, and repeated-theta reproducibility.
